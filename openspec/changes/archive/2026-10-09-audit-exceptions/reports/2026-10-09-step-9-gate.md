## Decisions taken by the implementer

- The guard reads the exceptions from `security/audit-exceptions.json` as
  `{ "exceptions": [ { id, package, reason, noFixEvidence, expires } ] }`, and an entry is in force through its own
  date and for 30 days after the day of the run at most.
- An advisory is matched by the pair (identifier GHSA, package); the packages that depend on it are not listed one by
  one.
- The production tree is read with `npm ls --omit=dev --all --json`, because the lock says what `npm ci` would
  install and the tree says what is installed, which is what the audit reads.
- `--today` exists so a test places itself on a date without touching the clock; the npm script never passes it.
- The tests read the guard as a module, so the suite has no child process and no socket; the command is what the gate
  runs.
- The fixtures of the base, of the head and of the production tree are the outputs of the day, saved as they were
  printed; the payloads that no real tree produces are derived from them, and the list of exceptions and the payload
  that is not an audit are written by hand.
